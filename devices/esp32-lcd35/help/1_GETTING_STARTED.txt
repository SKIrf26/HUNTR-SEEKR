1. GETTING STARTED
==================

What the board is
-----------------
The Waveshare ESP32-Touch-LCD-3.5 is a small board with a classic ESP32 chip (dual core, 16 MB flash, 2 MB extra memory), a 3.5 inch
capacitive touch screen (320 x 480, rounded corners), a built-in speaker and microphone, a TF (microSD) card slot, a battery
connector with a power chip, a real-time clock and a USB-C port. HUNTR/SEEKR is the software that runs on it.

Parts you may want to add (all optional)
----------------------------------------
  TF card      FAT32, 32 GB or smaller is safest. Fit it BEFORE you switch the board on (it is not noticed later).
               Logs, the ignore list and your own rules are kept on the card; with no card they go to the board's flash.
  Battery      A single-cell lithium battery on the board's battery connector. The board works from USB without one.
  GPS          A module wired to two free header pins (9600 baud). Set the two pin numbers (PIN_GPS_RX, PIN_GPS_TX) near the top of the
               sketch and build it yourself: the ready-made .bin has GPS switched off.
  Phone        Used for the Live view and to set the clock over the board's own Wi-Fi.

Step by step
------------
 1. Put the software on the board: help/2_FLASHING.txt.
 2. After the boot screen (it ends with "ready  Port Test 6") you land on the ARM page: eight group tiles
    (ALPR, AXON, CAMS, GLASS, DRONE, BT, TAGS, WIFI).
    Tap a tile to arm that group. Swipe left and right, or tap the tabs along the bottom, to move between ARM, FINDS, GROUPS, LOG and SETUP.
 3. Open FINDS to see what has been heard. Tap a device, then TRACK to follow its signal: the number closer to
    zero means closer.
 4. Open SETUP > Self-test. Every dot should be green. Motion is green and says "no sensor on this board", and Clock is amber until it is set (from your phone, over the board's Wi-Fi).
 5. Buttons: PWR (short press) turns the screen off and on, and a long press opens the Power screen (sleep, restart).
    BOOT (short press) shows the clock face; holding it puts the board to sleep. Touch or a button press wakes it.

Signal strength (dBm) is a rough guide to distance: closer to zero is closer. The board has one antenna and cannot tell
which direction a signal comes from, so walk toward the stronger reading.
